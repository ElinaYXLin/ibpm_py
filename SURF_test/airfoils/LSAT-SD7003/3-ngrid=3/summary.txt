SD7003 flowfield case, ngrid=3 (multi-domain far field) vs. ngrid=1 baseline
alpha=4.60 deg, Re=61100, dx=0.02 (nx=300, ny=152 -- bumped from 150 to
satisfy Grid.cc/grid.py's "ngrid>1 requires nx%4==0 and ny%4==0" assertion;
yoffset shifted to -1.52 to keep the domain centered), dt=0.005.

Context: this run investigates a mentor question about why 3-small_dt/'s
vorticity field still looks noisy after switching to native FFTW3. The
domain/resolution README (SD7003/README.md, "Limitations") documents that
this suite runs ngrid=1 (single uniform grid over a 6-chord domain) purely
for compute cost, even though the solver's own manual recommends
multi-domain (ngrid>1) for a domain this size. This run tests ngrid=3.

Initial conditions were checked against the UIUC LSAT dataset
(https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/uiuc_lsat.html) first: Re=61100 already
matches the LSAT SD7003.DRG.txt table's own stated average Reynolds number
exactly, and the impulsive-start-from-uniform-flow IC (py/ibpm.py:
x.omega.assign(0.0)) is standard for this solver and identical to the
unmodified C++ reference's IC -- so no IC changes were made or needed.

Result: ngrid=3 does NOT clean up the vorticity field. Both py/ibpm.py and
C++ build/ibpm produce broadband grid-scale speckle through t=20, same as
the ngrid=1 baseline (2-c++included/, 3-small_dt/) -- see flow_evolution.png,
columns t=0..20.

Also found: both implementations diverge to NaN shortly after t=20 at
ngrid=3 (first NaN at t=20.165 in py, t=20.945 in cpp). This was first
seen at dt=0.01 (same divergence time, ~t=20-21) and did NOT go away when
dt was halved to 0.005 -- ruling out a CFL/timestep cause. Since the
divergence time is essentially unchanged across a 2x change in dt, this
looks like a genuine multi-domain coupling instability specific to this
ngrid=3 configuration (SD7003, alpha=4.6, Re=61100, 6-chord domain), present
in both implementations, not a Python-port bug. flow_evolution.png shows
"diverged (NaN)" placeholders for t=25, t=30 accordingly.

Conclusion: ngrid=3 is not a fix for the vorticity-field noise reported by
the mentor within the window it stays stable (t<=20), and introduces a new
instability beyond that. The broadband speckle documented in
SD7003/README.md's "Limitations" section remains the best explanation:
under-resolved boundary layer at Re~61100 with dx=0.02-0.04 and no subgrid
dissipation, not a far-field/ngrid artifact.

What ngrid=3 actually does, and why it diverges (see instability_diagnostic.png)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
ngrid=N is Colonius & Taira 2007's multi-domain far-field scheme (doc/
ibpm_manual.tex sec. "Multi-domain method"; src/EllipticSolver.cc,
src/Scalar.cc): N nested grids share the *same* nx x ny point count, but
each successive (coarser) level covers 2x the physical domain of the one
inside it -- so ngrid=3 means level 0 is the requested domain (here
6x3.04c at dx=0.02), level 1 covers 12x6.08c at dx=0.04, and level 2
covers 24x12.16c at dx=0.08, all centered on the same origin. Every
elliptic solve (one Poisson + one Helmholtz per RK3 substep) runs
outside-in: solve the coarsest grid first with zero far-field BCs, then
Scalar::getBC() bilinearly interpolates that solution to supply boundary
conditions for the next-finer grid, and Scalar::coarsify() restricts
(4-point averaged) vorticity from fine to coarse grids to build each
level's forcing term. This lets the domain edge sit much farther from the
body without paying for a huge uniform grid.

Quantified the divergence using the restart snapshots already on disk
(no new runs needed) -- domain-RMS |omega| over time:
  ngrid=1 baseline (dt=0.01): 6.29 (t=5) -> 6.90 -> 7.75 -> 8.80 -> 8.59 -> 8.27 (t=30)
    -- saturates/plateaus, as expected for a statistically-stationary wake.
  ngrid=3 (dt=0.005):         6.61 (t=5) -> 8.33 -> 11.13 -> 27.19 (t=20) -> NaN
    -- grows without bound instead of saturating (panel A).
Splitting the ngrid=3 field into a "core" band (near body/wake,
|x|<1.5,|y|<0.5) and an "edge" band (outermost 3 cells of the finest grid,
i.e. right at the level-0/level-1 interface): the edge band starts far
below the core (2.4 vs 15.4 at t=5) and catches all the way up to it by
t=20 (27.7 vs 30.0) -- panel B. That growth pattern pinpoints where the
runaway energy comes from: this suite's broadband grid-scale vorticity
noise (already documented to spread through the whole domain over time,
SD7003/README.md "Limitations") eventually reaches the fine-grid boundary
and starts being repeatedly round-tripped through coarsify()/getBC()'s
bilinear restriction/interpolation every RK3 substep. Those operators are
plain local averaging, not a clean low-pass filter, so they can alias
high-wavenumber broadband content between grid levels; with no subgrid
dissipation to damp it, the aliased energy compounds every step until it
blows up. This also explains why halving dt didn't move the divergence
time: the bottleneck is the physical/advective time for noise to reach the
domain edge and begin compounding through the interface, not a per-step
truncation-error threshold (which is what dt-dependent CFL blowup would
look like).

Why dx=0.01 shows larger vorticity fluctuations (see 3-dx0.01/summary.txt
for the run details; stats also in instability_diagnostic.png panel C)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Comparing the dx=0.02 baseline and dx=0.01 fields at the same matched
snapshot (t=30, both ngrid=1): max|omega| grows from 208 to 369, but
domain-RMS |omega| is essentially flat (8.27 vs 7.62 -- if anything
slightly lower). So the "larger fluctuations" visible in flow_evolution.png
are peak-value spikes, not more total energy: a finer grid can represent
sharper, more concentrated vortical structures (thinner shear layers,
tighter vortex cores) that dx=0.02 simply smooths over by having fewer
points to represent the same gradient. Because there's no subgrid
dissipation and the boundary layer at Re~61100 isn't resolved at either
dx=0.02 or dx=0.01, refining the grid doesn't converge the peak vorticity
toward a fixed value -- it keeps sharpening whatever gradients the grid can
now represent. This is the same underlying pathology documented in
SD7003/README.md's "Limitations" section (under-resolved boundary layer,
no subgrid dissipation), viewed from the resolution axis instead of the
far-field-domain axis.
